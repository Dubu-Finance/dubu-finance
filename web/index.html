What this does and does not say.
  1. Both venues hold the same token inventory, so the difference in the top table is the pricing mechanism — plus, for UniV2, however far its internal price has drifted from the market since it was last traded. Those are separated below each table for exactly that reason.
  2. At small size most of the honest gap is fees, not curve shape: UniV2 charges a flat 30 bp where this pool charges a 5 bp half-spread. The curve only starts to matter at size.
  3. Past its capacity epoch this pool refuses — it quotes nothing and the swap reverts — where UniV2 keeps filling at any size. Declining is not strictly better than filling badly. It is a different trade-off, and it is what bounds how much a stale ladder can be picked off for.
  4. Adverse selection is not modelled. The reference is right by assumption here. These are execution-quality numbers for an honest taker, not a claim about the maker's P&L.
Both tokens are ours and both venues were seeded by us — GIWA has no canonical stablecoin, so there is no third-party liquidity to compare against. This measures curves, not markets.
