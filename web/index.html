What this comparison does and does not say. Both venues hold the same token inventory and are measured against the same reference mid, so the difference is the pricing mechanism and nothing else. Four things it is not:
  1. At small size almost none of the gap is the curve — UniV2 charges a flat 30 bp fee against our 5 bp half-spread, and that is essentially the whole difference.
  2. The largest ratio is not a headline number. It is the biggest trade that still fits inside one capacity epoch, where the constant-product impact has compounded and ours has not.
  3. Past the epoch capacity this pool refuses — it quotes 0 and the swap reverts — where UniV2 keeps filling at any size. A venue that declines is not strictly better than one that fills badly. It is a different trade-off, and that bound is what limits how much a stale ladder can be picked off for.
  4. Adverse selection is not modelled. The reference mid is right by assumption here. These are execution-quality numbers for an honest taker, not a claim about the market maker's P&L.
Both tokens are ours and both venues were seeded by us, because GIWA has no canonical stablecoin. This measures curves, not markets.
